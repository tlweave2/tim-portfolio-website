About Me

I came to software late. Before writing production code I spent 17 years as the owner of Strokers, a motorcycle service business in Merced, CA, and before that I was a clinical engineer maintaining medical equipment at Stanford University Hospital and Walter Reed Army Medical Center.

I came back to school in my 40s, finished a B.S. in Software Engineering at ASU with a 3.83 GPA, and I'm now working through a Master of Computer Science with an AI concentration. The business and engineering background informs how I approach software: I think about what actually needs to work, for whom, and what breaks first under real load.

These days I work on AI and full-stack development. Two of the things I've built, VidlyAI and useventful.com, are live products with paying customers rather than demos, and I handle their infrastructure and support myself.

Clinical engineering: the PiNO system

At Stanford I assembled and calibrated a portable inhaled nitric oxide delivery system (PiNO) for neonates. It went through FDA evaluation and a multicenter trial whose results were published in the New England Journal of Medicine. Working on equipment where a failure reaches a patient set my expectations for what reliability means, and I have not really adjusted them since.
